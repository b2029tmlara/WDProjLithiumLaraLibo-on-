Your Piano Practice Plan

Hello, ! Welcome to your piano practice plan! Below is a list of things for you to follow or use as a basis on your journey to become a better piano player.

  • Skill Level: Since you are a Focus on curved finger posture, 5-finger patterns, and the "thumb-tuck" for one-octave scales to build a strong mechanical foundation.
    Then, we suggest that you prioritize two-octave scales/arpeggios and "syncopated pedaling" to ensure your playing sounds clean and professional. When you;re finally used to everything, we encourage trying more advanced techniques such as incorporating scales in 3rds and 6ths, and focus on tone production using arm weight to master high-level artistry.
  • Preferred Genre: As a fan of ,Work on "voicing" (making the melody sing over the accompaniment) and mastering polyphonic textures.
    If you'd like, you could also focus on chord inversions, the I-V-vi-IV progression, and using rhythmic "grooves" in your left hand.
    You could also dedicate some time to the Blues scale, II-V-I progressions, and experimenting with "swung" vs. "straight" eighth notes, should you pursue jazz.
  • Practice Time: With a daily practice time of hours, we suggest breaking it down into focused sessions to avoid fatigue and maximize "muscle memory" retention:
    Session Segment Duration Focus Area
    Technical Warm-up 15% of time Scales, arpeggios, and Hanon/Czerny exercises.
    Sight-Reading 10% of time Reading 2 levels below your current grade; no stopping!
    Repertoire Work 50% of time Deep practice in small 2-4 measure chunks.
    Genre/Creative 25% of time Improvising, ear training, or lead sheet playing.
